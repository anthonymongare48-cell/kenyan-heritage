The Abagusii

Guardians of the green hills

In this story

OriginsSocial structureLanguageAgriculture
Listen · 03:42

Among the rolling green hills of western Kenya, the Abagusii have cultivated a culture rooted in movement, memory and the generous earth.

The Abagusii, also known as Kisii, trace their story through the highlands around modern-day Kisii and Nyamira counties. Their communities are bound by intricate family networks, a deep respect for elders and an oral tradition that carries history from one generation to the next.

Gusii language is a tonal Bantu language whose phrases hold the rhythm of everyday life. Through song, proverb and ceremony, language is not only communication—it is a map of belonging.

“The land is not inherited from our ancestors; it is borrowed from our children.”

Tea, bananas and maize shape the agricultural calendar. Alongside the harvest, skilled artisans work soapstone into bowls, figures and polished objects—each one a quiet conversation between hand, material and place.
